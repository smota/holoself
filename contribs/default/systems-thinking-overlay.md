# Systems Thinking Overlay — Complex Problem Analysis

## What it does

Prevents first-level, common-sense answers to problems that involve feedback loops, multiple stakeholders, or organizational change. Forces analysis of second-order consequences and leverage points before a recommendation is formed.

## When to apply

Apply before recommending any course of action when the problem involves:
- Multiple stakeholders with divergent interests
- Organizational or structural change
- Second-order consequences (what the solution causes, not just what it solves)
- Situations where the obvious answer has a track record of not working

Do not apply to: well-bounded technical problems, simple decisions with no downstream effects, or requests for straightforward information.

## How to apply

Before forming a recommendation, run this diagnostic:

**1. Map the feedback loops**
What does this decision reinforce or dampen? Where does it loop back to create unintended consequences? Identify both reinforcing loops (accelerators) and balancing loops (constraints).

**2. Identify unintended consequences**
What does the proposed solution optimize for — and what does it make worse? Who benefits and who absorbs the cost? What does it signal to stakeholders who are watching but not directly involved?

**3. Find leverage points**
Where in the system is a small change capable of producing a large effect? Leverage is usually not at the obvious intervention point — it is upstream, in the structure or the information flows.

**4. Flag the non-answers**
Before recommending, explicitly name the common-sense response and explain why it is insufficient for this level of complexity. This surfaces the diagnostic reasoning rather than burying it.

## Output convention

In the final output, the Systems Thinking analysis does not appear as a framework. It appears as depth — as insight that a first-level answer would have missed. Flag complexity where relevant: "the risk here is not the decision itself, but what it signals to..." or "the constraint is upstream of the obvious intervention point..."

## Integration note

Systems Thinking informs the insight. The Pyramid structures it into an argument. ABT shapes it into a narrative. The analysis happens before the output is structured — not during.
