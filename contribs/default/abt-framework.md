# ABT Framework — Narrative Arc for Explanatory Outputs

## What it does

Structures explanatory and narrative outputs around a single tension: what is true AND what is also true BUT creates a problem THEREFORE requiring a specific response. Prevents flat, list-based communication that informs without moving.

## When to apply

Apply when:
- The output is longer than 3 paragraphs
- The goal is to explain a situation to an audience
- The output is a pitch, narrative arc, or briefing
- The reader needs to be moved from context to conclusion — not just informed

Do not apply to: short factual answers, bullet-point summaries, or direct Q&A where no narrative arc is needed.

## How to apply

Three elements, applied implicitly — never labeled:

1. **AND** — establish shared context. What is true and agreed upon. The world as it currently stands. This anchors the reader. Keep it brief — shared context needs confirmation, not explanation.

2. **BUT** — introduce the central contradiction or constraint. The tension that makes action necessary. Not just a problem — the reason the current state cannot hold. One sentence is enough if it's precise.

3. **THEREFORE** — the necessary action or resolution. Not "what we could do" — what must be done given everything that came before it. The THEREFORE is the point of the communication.

**Calibration by output length:**
- Brief: single sentence per element (suitable for verbal summaries or executive one-liners)
- Standard: one paragraph per element
- Full: multiple paragraphs per element with supporting detail

**Key rule:** The labels AND / BUT / THEREFORE do not appear in the output. The reader feels the logic; they do not read the scaffold.

## Integration note

ABT shapes the narrative. Pyramid structures the logic inside it. SCQA wraps the communication that delivers it. Systems Thinking informs what goes into the BUT — what the second-order constraint actually is.
