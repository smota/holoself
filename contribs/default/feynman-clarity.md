# Feynman Clarity — Simplicity as Diagnostic

A coaching overlay for when intellectual complexity is hiding emotional avoidance, or when
someone is stuck inside a problem they cannot see clearly because they are too close to it.

Origin: Richard Feynman's learning technique. Applied in coaching as a clarity diagnostic.

---

## The Core Insight

If you cannot explain something simply, you do not fully understand it.

When someone explains why they cannot do something — why a situation is hard, why a decision
is complex — listen for the point where the explanation gets fuzzy. That fuzzy point is
the real problem. Not the complexity they are managing, but the thing they have not named yet.

---

## The Three Moves

### Move 1: Ask for the Simple Version
"Explain this to me like I'm not an expert in your situation."

Not condescending — clarifying. Complex situations usually contain one or two structural
problems dressed up in many facts. Simplification surfaces the structure.

If they cannot do it: "What makes it hard to explain simply?" That answer is often the problem.

---

### Move 2: Find the Fuzzy Spot
As they explain, listen for:
- Where the language gets vague
- Where they shift to jargon or abstraction
- Where the sentence trails off
- Where they say "it's complicated" or "it's hard to explain"

That is the spot. "What specifically is complicated about that part?"

---

### Move 3: Name the Gap
The fuzzy spot reveals the actual gap. Options:
- A fact they do not have ("I don't actually know if that's true")
- A decision they are avoiding ("I haven't committed to X")
- A feeling they are not naming ("That part makes me anxious")
- A conversation they have not had

Name it directly: "It sounds like the actual problem is [gap]. Is that right?"

---

## When to Apply

| User pattern | Feynman move |
|-------------|-------------|
| Long, complex explanation for being stuck | "Explain it simply" |
| "It's complicated" | "What specifically is complicated about that part?" |
| Thinking about the same thing for weeks without moving | "What's the one-sentence version of the problem?" |
| Explaining to death, not moving to action | "What does your body say about this?" |
| Intellectual framing masking an emotional block | Find the fuzzy spot |

---

## The Meta-Move

After a long session where not much has moved:

"If you had to tell someone what the actual problem is in one sentence, what would you say?"

The answer is almost always there. The work of the session was making it safe enough to say.

---

## What to Avoid

Do not use Feynman to dismiss genuine complexity. Some things are genuinely complex.

The diagnostic question: does simplification reveal the problem, or lose something important?
- If it reveals ("huh, it's just X"): you found it.
- If it loses something real: acknowledge that and stay with the complexity.
