# Enneagram — Type Awareness for Coaching

Personality framework based on 9 core motivations. Useful when a behavioral pattern keeps
recurring despite naming it — the type often explains the structure beneath the pattern.

---

## The 9 Types: Core Motivation and Coaching Signal

| Type | Name | Core Fear | Core Desire | Coaching pattern to watch |
|------|------|-----------|-------------|--------------------------|
| 1 | The Reformer | Being wrong/corrupt | Integrity, improvement | Perfectionism as delay; harsh self-criticism after misses |
| 2 | The Helper | Being unloved | Being loved | Overgiving as avoidance of own work; needs from others go unnamed |
| 3 | The Achiever | Being worthless | Being valuable | Conflating doing with being; image management over substance |
| 4 | The Individualist | Having no identity | Being authentic | Staying in emotion; uniqueness as exemption from standard demands |
| 5 | The Investigator | Being useless | Being competent | Over-researching before action; withholding in groups |
| 6 | The Loyalist | Having no support | Security | Analysis paralysis; catastrophizing; testing others |
| 7 | The Enthusiast | Being trapped/in pain | Freedom, satisfaction | Starting without finishing; optimism over accountability |
| 8 | The Challenger | Being controlled | Self-protection | Dominating; pushing through resistance by force |
| 9 | The Peacemaker | Loss/separation | Inner stability | Merging with others' priorities; avoiding conflict even with themselves |

## Stress and Growth Directions

Under pressure, each type shifts toward a stress arrow. At their best, toward growth.

| Type | Under Stress → looks like | At Best → looks like |
|------|--------------------------|----------------------|
| 1 | 4 — moody, irrational | 7 — playful, accepting |
| 2 | 8 — aggressive, controlling | 4 — self-aware, emotionally honest |
| 3 | 9 — disengaged, apathetic | 6 — loyal, cooperative |
| 4 | 2 — clinging, dependent | 1 — disciplined, principled |
| 5 | 7 — scattered, impulsive | 8 — decisive, confident |
| 6 | 3 — competitive, image-focused | 9 — relaxed, trusting |
| 7 | 1 — critical, perfectionistic | 5 — focused, deep |
| 8 | 5 — withdrawn, secretive | 2 — open, caring |
| 9 | 6 — anxious, reactive | 3 — energized, goal-directed |

## Coaching Application

**If the type is known:**
- Use the core fear to understand what patterns protect them from
- Stress behavior explains regression under high pressure — look for it
- Growth direction points to what integration looks like for them specifically

**If the type is unknown:**
- Do not diagnose. Watch for the pattern that persists across different contexts — that's the structure.
- Common misidentifications: 3s appear as 1s (they care about success, not correctness); 6s appear as 2s (loyalty over helpfulness); 9s appear as 7s (avoidance over enthusiasm).

**Key question per type:**

| Type | The question that cuts |
|------|----------------------|
| 1 | "What would it look like if this were good enough?" |
| 2 | "What do you need here — separate from what they need?" |
| 3 | "What are you working toward that no one else would call achievement?" |
| 4 | "What would you do if this situation weren't special?" |
| 5 | "What do you know that you're not yet acting on?" |
| 6 | "What's the worst case — and could you handle it?" |
| 7 | "What do you want to be able to say you finished?" |
| 8 | "What's the version of this where you don't have to win?" |
| 9 | "What do you actually want, separate from what everyone else wants?" |

## Integration with Default Frameworks

Enneagram informs the behavioral patterns section of a session. When a pattern recurs
(from `patterns.md`), the type structure often explains why the pattern is sticky.

- Type 3 + "building as hiding" pattern: the building is achievement proxy.
- Type 6 + "aggressive planning" pattern: planning manages anxiety, not output.
- Type 1 + "perfection as delay" pattern: the standard is the protection.

Name the structure. Then use the standard coaching moves (First 2 Minutes, Mochary ISP, etc.)
