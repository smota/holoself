# Nonviolent Communication (NVC)

Framework developed by Marshall Rosenberg. Apply when coaching around difficult conversations,
conflict, or any situation where emotional content is obscuring what the person actually needs.

---

## The Four Components

### 1. Observation
What happened — stated without evaluation or interpretation.

- "You were 20 minutes late to the meeting" — observation
- "You're irresponsible" — evaluation
- "You always do this" — generalization (not observation)

**In coaching:** Help the user separate what they observed from the story they told about it.
"What actually happened — just the facts?"

---

### 2. Feeling
The actual emotional experience — not a thought dressed as a feeling.

- "I felt dismissed" — not a feeling (contains interpretation of another's intent)
- "I felt hurt / embarrassed / anxious / relieved" — feeling
- "I feel like you don't care" — not a feeling (contains a thought about another person)

**The tell:** "I feel *like*..." is almost always a thought. "I feel [emotion word]..." is a feeling.

**In coaching:** "What are you *feeling* — not what are you thinking about them?"

---

### 3. Need
The underlying human need that is met or unmet. Feelings point to needs.

Core needs: autonomy, connection, contribution, safety, recognition, clarity, respect,
fairness, competence, consistency, rest, play.

**In coaching:** "What do you need that you're not getting?" or "What was unmet when that happened?"

This is the layer most people skip. They jump from observation to request without naming
the need — which is why requests often do not land or feel coercive.

---

### 4. Request
Concrete, doable, present-tense. Not a demand.

A demand uses implicit force to get compliance. A request leaves the other person's autonomy
intact — "no" is a valid answer, and the relationship can survive it.

- "I'd like you to let me know by end of day whether you can make the meeting" — request
- "You need to communicate better" — vague, and demand
- "You should have told me" — past-focused, not actionable

**In coaching:** "What specifically do you want to ask them? Can you say it in one sentence?"

---

## Coaching Application

**Use NVC when:**
- User has a conversation coming up that they are avoiding or dreading
- User is stuck in blame or resentment and cannot move to action
- User's requests of someone else keep failing or escalating
- User is not sure what they actually want from a situation

**The coaching move:**
Walk through the four components about the situation. Do not force the user to use NVC
language in their actual conversation — use it as a diagnostic to find what is real underneath.

**The most useful component in coaching: Need.**
Most people in conflict do not know what they need. Finding the need often dissolves
the stuck loop — because the request that follows actually matches the situation.

---

## Quick Diagnostic Questions

- "What did you observe — just the facts, no interpretation?"
- "What are you feeling? Just the emotion word."
- "What do you need that you're not getting?"
- "What's the one concrete thing you would want to ask for?"
