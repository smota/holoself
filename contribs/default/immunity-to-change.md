# Immunity to Change — Kegan & Lahey

Diagnostic framework for understanding why behavior does not change despite genuine commitment.
From "Immunity to Change" (Kegan & Lahey, 2009).

---

## The Core Idea

When someone commits to change and repeatedly fails, it is rarely a skill or motivation problem.
It is an immune system — a set of competing commitments and hidden assumptions that actively
protects against the change. The system is not broken; it is doing exactly what it was designed to do.

---

## The Four-Column Diagnostic

| Column 1 | Column 2 | Column 3 | Column 4 |
|----------|----------|----------|----------|
| Commitment | Doing / Not Doing | Competing Commitment | Big Assumption |
| What I say I want | What I do that works against it | What I'm also committed to (hidden) | The belief that makes Column 3 feel necessary |

**Example:**

| C1 | C2 | C3 | C4 |
|----|----|----|-----|
| Give more direct feedback | Softening criticism, avoiding hard conversations | Keeping peace; not being seen as harsh | "If I'm fully direct, people will think I'm cruel and pull away" |

---

## Walking the Diagnostic

**Column 1 — The commitment:**
Ask: "What do you want to change or improve?" Write it as a positive commitment: "I am committed to X."

**Column 2 — The counter-behavior:**
Ask: "What are you doing, or not doing, that works against this commitment?"
Be specific. Observable actions, not interpretations.

**Column 3 — The hidden commitment:**
Ask: "Imagine you were going to do the opposite of Column 2 consistently. What would feel
scary or problematic about that?"

The answer reveals the competing commitment. It is usually protection of something:
safety, status, approval, control, belonging. It is not a flaw — it is a value operating covertly.

**Column 4 — The big assumption:**
Ask: "What do you assume is true about yourself or the world that makes the Column 3
commitment feel necessary?"

This is the belief that must be true for the immune system to make sense. It is often a
generalization from past experience that is no longer situationally accurate.

---

## Key Distinctions

Column 3 is not a psychological failing. It is a protection system built for good reason.
The frame is: "Of course you're not changing — look at what you're protecting against."

The big assumption was often *true* in an earlier context. The problem is that it still
runs on autopilot in contexts where it no longer applies.

---

## What to Do With the Diagnosis

Do not argue against the big assumption. Design a safe experiment to test it.

"What small action would let you test whether this assumption is actually true in your
current situation?"

The experiment must be small enough to be safe, specific enough to produce real data,
and run in the actual context (not a hypothetical).

Running the experiment — not debating the assumption — is what shifts the immune system.

---

## When to Apply

- Commitment to change has been made and broken multiple times (not once)
- User says they "can't" do something they intellectually want to do
- Pattern has continued despite strategy changes
- User is frustrated at themselves for repeating the same behavior
