# Internal Family Systems (IFS) — Parts Work

Model developed by Richard Schwartz. Apply when a behavioral pattern persists despite the user
naming and committing to change it — the pattern is being run by a *part*, not a failure of will.

---

## The Core Model

The mind contains multiple sub-personalities (parts), each with a role and a positive intent.
The job is not to eliminate parts — it is to unburden them so they can step back.

**Self** is not a part. It is the calm, compassionate, curious center. When coaching from Self,
the person is curious about their parts rather than fused with them. Self shows up as the
4 Cs: Calm, Curious, Compassionate, Clear.

---

## Three Part Types

### Managers
Protectors that control before anything bad can happen.

- Show up as: perfectionism, over-preparation, people-pleasing, harsh self-criticism, workaholism
- Positive intent: protect from shame, rejection, or failure
- Coaching signal: "I should..." / "I have to..." / "I can't let that happen" / excessive justification

### Firefighters
Reactive protectors — they respond after Exiles get activated. Fast suppression.

- Show up as: procrastination, scrolling, impulsive decisions, overeating, overworking
- Positive intent: stop pain immediately, before it lands
- Coaching signal: sudden avoidance of something recently fine; behavior that happens "before I realized it"

### Exiles
The wounded parts that carry pain, shame, or fear — often from earlier in life.
Managers and Firefighters exist to keep Exiles locked away.

- Coaching signal: outsized emotional reaction; disproportionate shame; "I just feel worthless when this happens"

---

## Coaching Application

IFS in coaching is not therapy. Use it to name structure, not excavate the wound.

**When you see a persistent pattern (3+ sessions, same loop):**

1. Name that a part seems to be involved — don't diagnose: "It sounds like part of you wants X, and another part keeps stopping you."
2. Get curious about the part's intent: "What is that part trying to protect you from?"
3. Acknowledge the positive intent: "That part has been doing this job for a long time. Makes sense."
4. Ask what it needs: "What would need to be true for that part to step back a little?"

**Recognizing Manager behavior:**
- Justifications for why action isn't possible yet
- Self-criticism after misses that exceeds what the situation warrants
- Need for more preparation before starting

**Recognizing Firefighter behavior:**
- Topic-jumping mid-session
- Sudden fatigue or disengagement
- "I know I should but I just... don't"

**The key reframe for the user:**
Not "why can't you just do it" → "what part of you is making this feel unsafe, and what does it need?"

---

## When to Apply

- Same pattern has persisted after naming and committing (3+ sessions)
- Shame appears disproportionate to the situation
- User is in genuine inner conflict ("I want to do X but I also don't")
- User self-attacks after misses beyond what is situationally warranted

Do not push IFS when the user is in practical problem-solving mode. It is a tool for when
the emotional structure is the obstacle, not the tactical path.
