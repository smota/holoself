# Stoic Frame — Redirecting from Uncontrollable Outcomes

A coaching overlay — a lens that shifts how the coach engages with the user's situation.
Apply when the user is spending significant energy on factors outside their control, or when
fear of outcomes is blocking action.

Origin: Marcus Aurelius, Epictetus, Seneca. Applied through Ryan Holiday and modern practice.

---

## Core Distinctions

### Dichotomy of Control
Epictetus: "Some things are in our control and others are not."

In our control: opinions, intentions, what we pursue, what we avoid, how we respond.
Not in our control: results, others' reactions, market conditions, timing, luck.

**Coaching use:** When stuck on outcomes — "what if they say no," "what if the market turns,"
"what if I fail." Redirect to what they control: the quality of the work, the ask,
the preparation, the response.

**The question:** "What part of this is actually in your control?" Then: "What's your move on that part?"

---

### Negative Visualization (Premeditatio Malorum)
Imagine the worst case — not to cause dread, but to drain its power.

**Coaching use:** When fear of a specific outcome is preventing action. "Walk me through the
worst case. What actually happens? Then what? Could you handle it?"

The answer is almost always yes — and the fear was larger than the thing. Once named fully,
worst cases lose the vagueness that makes them frightening.

---

### Amor Fati
Love of fate — not resigned acceptance, but active embrace of what has already happened.

"Don't seek for everything to happen as you wish it would — wish that everything happens as
it actually does. Then your life will flow well." — Epictetus

**Coaching use:** After a setback. Not "that's fine" — but "given that this happened,
what's the move that uses it?" The obstacle is not merely an obstacle — it is material.

**The question:** "What does this make possible that wasn't possible before?"

---

### Memento Mori
Remember that you will die. Not morbid — clarifying.

**Coaching use (use sparingly):** When priorities are scattered or small concerns are taking
space that large ones should occupy. "In five years, will you remember this? What will you
wish you had spent this time on?"

Use at most once per session, and only when genuinely warranted. It is not a rhetorical move.

---

## When to Apply

| User pattern | Stoic move |
|-------------|------------|
| Spiraling on what others think | Dichotomy of control |
| Delaying because outcome is uncertain | Negative visualization |
| Priorities scattered, many things feel urgent | Memento mori |
| Stuck after a failure or setback | Amor fati |
| Fear of failure blocking clear action | "What's actually in your control here?" |

---

## What Stoicism Is Not

Not detachment. Not "nothing matters." Not suppression of emotion.

The Stoics were fully engaged — Marcus Aurelius led Rome. The point is directed engagement:
give full energy to what you can change; release what you cannot. This is not passivity.
It is the elimination of wasted motion.
