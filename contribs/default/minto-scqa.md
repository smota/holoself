# Minto SCQA — Narrative Flow for Written Communication

## What it does

Structures emails and meeting invitations so the reader moves from shared reality to necessary action without ever noticing the framework. The logic is visible; the scaffold is not.

## When to apply

- Drafting or rewriting emails
- Writing meeting invitations
- Any written communication where the goal is to move a reader from context to decision or action

Do not apply to: outputs that lead with analysis (→ Pyramid), real-time dialogue, or bullet-point summaries.

## How to apply

Four elements, woven into prose — never labeled:

1. **Situation** — the shared reality the reader already accepts. One or two sentences that establish common ground without debate. "We agreed on X. The programme is in Y phase."

2. **Complication** — the catalyst that creates the need to act. Not a problem, necessarily — the tension between the Situation and what must happen next. "What has changed / what we've since learned / what the constraint is."

3. **Question** — the question the Complication raises in the reader's mind. Usually implied, not written. The writer should feel it but the reader derives it. "So what do we do?"

4. **Answer** — the resolution, recommendation, or ask. Stated directly. No hedging. This is the point of the communication.

**Key rule:** The SCQA structure must be invisible in the final output. Do not use headers, labels, or transitional phrases that signal the framework ("That said...", "The challenge is...", "My recommendation is..."). The prose flows; the logic is felt.

## Integration note

SCQA governs the communication layer. When a Pyramid argument or ABT narrative is also present, SCQA wraps the outer communication — it determines how the message opens and lands, while Pyramid or ABT structures the content inside.
